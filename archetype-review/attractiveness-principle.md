# Review: Attractiveness Principle

Card: [`systems-thinking/archetypes/attractiveness-principle.md`](../systems-thinking/archetypes/attractiveness-principle.md)  
Chapters: 14  
Attribution on the card: Named after Jay Forrester's argument in Urban Dynamics (1969) that growth is ultimately controlled by controlling attractiveness; treated in archetype collections as a variation of Limits to Growth

**Summary on the card:** Limits to Growth with several different limits at once, each biting at a different size; relieving one only lets growth run until the next binds, so the real decision is which limits to relieve and which kinds of unattractiveness to accept on purpose.

**Diagnostic question:** Is growth running into several different limits at once, more than can be relieved, so that fixing one just hands growth to the next?

## The card's own description (check it agrees with the structure)

> One reinforcing growth loop and several balancing loops, each with one minus sign, each a different *kind* of limit that starts to bite at a different size, some after a delay. In the book's example: training staff spread thin (support per member), full bunks (crowding), and accumulating attention from outside authorities (scrutiny). Each responds to a different fix, and some (scrutiny) can't be bought off at all.
