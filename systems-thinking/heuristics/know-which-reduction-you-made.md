title: Know which reduction you just made
summary: Every model, archetype, boundary and summary is a reduction; the discipline isn't avoiding reduction but knowing which one you made, drawing what it left out, and watching what pushes back.
chapters: [26, 1]
when: Presenting or acting on any model, diagram or one-line conclusion.
then: State what the reduction left out (the boundary's exclusions, the unmeasured parameters, the parties not consulted) and plan to look for pushback there.

A model that refused to reduce anything would be as large as the system it described, and useless for the same reason. The book's one-sentence summary of systems thinking (ask, every time, what structure is producing this behavior, and draw it before trusting any answer that skipped the sketch) is itself a reduction, which is the Second Law's point.
