---
id: commons-need-collective-rules
type: heuristic
title: A commons needs rules its users can see kept
summary: Over-use of a shared resource can't be fixed by one user's restraint; it takes rules every user agrees to, can see being kept and can enforce, whether set by a regulator or by the users themselves.
chapters: [13]
when: Several teams or actors draw down a shared resource nobody owns (a cluster, a reviewer's time, on-call attention, a shared database, a fishery).
then: Propose a visible allocation rule with monitoring and enforcement that the users themselves accept, or meter the resource so each user's draw comes back to them promptly. Don't rely on appeals to restraint.
links:
  counters: [tragedy-of-the-commons]
attribution: Elinor Ostrom, Governing the Commons (1990)
series: [competing-for-shared-resources]
---

Unilateral restraint only cedes the share to users who don't restrain themselves, so it fails even when everyone understands the danger. Ostrom's evidence is that communities often design workable governance without outside authority, as long as the rules are agreed, observable and enforceable.
