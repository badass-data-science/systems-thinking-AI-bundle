id: snapshot-test
type: heuristic
title: Snapshot test
summary: To tell a stock from a flow, imagine stopping time and photographing the system; whatever you could still count or measure in the photograph is a stock, and whatever only makes sense as a rate is a flow.
chapters: [16, 4]
attribution: John Sterman, Business Dynamics (2000)
when: Classifying any quantity in a model, especially when translating a CLD into stocks and flows.
then: Freeze time. Countable now (inventory, headcount, backlog, attention, belief) means stock. Meaningful only per unit time (hiring, shipping, scrap, growth) means flow. Neither (a ratio, a computed level) means auxiliary.
links:
  diagnoses: [stock, flow]
  related: [translate-cld-to-stock-and-flow]
---

A photograph can't show a speed, which is the whole test. "Effective stockpile growth" fails it, so it becomes the stockpile's pipes; the stockpile itself survives it.
