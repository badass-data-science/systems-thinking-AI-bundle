## Concepts (53)

- [Actor-based modeling (ABM)](concepts/actor-based-modeling.md) (Ch. 3): A simulation paradigm in which code for many individually simulated actors, each following local rules, is run forward to see what group behavior emerges.
- [Agentic AI](concepts/agentic-ai.md) (Ch. 3): Real AI systems (a coding assistant, a research agent, a multi-agent pipeline) taking autonomous action, sometimes for long stretches with no human checking in.
- [AI system as a system](concepts/ai-system-as-a-system.md) (Ch. 20): An AI agent's usage, the trust accumulating around its output, and the scrutiny that trust invites or discourages form a real feedback structure with stocks, flows and loops, to which every systems tool applies directly.
- [Analytical thinking](concepts/analytical-thinking.md) (Ch. 1): Decomposing a problem into isolated components, finding the component that misbehaves, and fixing it; powerful for understanding parts, blind to behavior that comes from how parts interact.
- [Anchoring](concepts/anchoring.md) (Ch. 18): The pull an early number, even an arbitrary first-draft guess, keeps exerting over every later estimate long after better information exists.
- [Auxiliary variable](concepts/auxiliary.md) (Ch. 16): In a stock-and-flow model, a quantity that is neither a stock nor a flow; recalculated each time step from other quantities, it sets how far a flow's valve opens, and constants (capacities, targets, yields) are drawn the same way.
- [Availability heuristic](concepts/availability-heuristic.md) (Ch. 18): Overweighting whatever example comes to mind most easily, whether or not it's typical.
- [Balancing loop](concepts/balancing-loop.md) (Ch. 8): A feedback loop with an odd number of negative links, which compares a current state to a reference point and takes corrective action sized to the gap, so it seeks and then defends a goal it never sets.
- [Boundary critique](concepts/boundary-critique.md) (Ch. 25): Asking not only what is inside a model's boundary but whose worldview that boundary needs buy-in from before anyone will act on it, and whether those stakeholders were consulted while it was drawn or only informed afterward.
- [Causal loop diagram (CLD)](concepts/causal-loop-diagram.md) (Ch. 6): A picture of feedback structure with four ingredients: nodes that are quantities, cause-to-effect arrows marked + or -, R/B loop labels, and double-hash delay marks.
- [Chaos theory](concepts/chaos-theory.md) (Ch. 3): The study of how simple, fully specified deterministic systems can still be unpredictable over the long run, because tiny differences in starting points grow.
- [Co-flow](concepts/co-flow.md) (Ch. 16): A second stock, with its own pipes, that shadows a main stock to track one attribute of what it holds, so that dividing the shadow by the main stock gives that attribute's share.
- [Complexity science](concepts/complexity-science.md) (Ch. 3): The study of how large numbers of simple, interacting actors following local rules produce coordinated behavior that nobody specified.
- [Confirmation bias](concepts/confirmation-bias.md) (Ch. 18): The pull toward evidence, and toward modeling choices, that confirm what you already believed.
- [Cybernetics](concepts/cybernetics.md) (Ch. 2): The study of control, named by Norbert Wiener in 1948: how a system uses feedback about its own output to keep that output on target.
- [Cynefin framework](concepts/cynefin-framework.md) (Ch. 21): Dave Snowden's framework sorting a decision by how cause and effect behave in it: clear (best practice), complicated (expert analysis finds a knowable answer), complex (coherent only in retrospect, so probe safely) and chaotic (act first to stabilize), with disorder at the center for not yet knowing which.
- [Delay](concepts/delay.md) (Ch. 9): A link in a feedback loop where the effect lags the cause, either as an information delay (a lag in finding out) or a material delay (a lag in the world changing once everyone knows).
- [Emily's Laws of System Complexity](concepts/emilys-laws-of-system-complexity.md) (Ch. 1): First Law: for every reduction, there is a greater and opposite clusterfuck. Second Law: the first law is a reductionist statement.
- [Feedback loop](concepts/feedback-loop.md) (Ch. 6): A causal chain that curls back on itself (A affects B, B affects C, C affects A), so a system's output becomes an input to its own future output; every such loop is either reinforcing or balancing.
- [First-, second- and third-order effects](concepts/second-order-effects.md) (Ch. 22): A discipline of asking "and then what?" more than once, deliberately, stopping at the order where the causal chain gets too thin to defend; a real first-order gain and a real second-order risk can both be true of one change.
- [Flow](concepts/flow.md) (Ch. 4): A rate, the speed at which a stock is filling or draining per unit of time, with no inertia of its own.
- [General systems theory](concepts/general-systems-theory.md) (Ch. 2): Ludwig von Bertalanffy's search for one vocabulary that could describe any system of any kind (open systems, hierarchy, equifinality).
- [Goal-seeking behavior](concepts/goal-seeking-behavior.md) (Ch. 8): The signature curve of a balancing loop without significant delay: the state approaches its goal quickly at first, then more and more gently, because the correction is sized to a shrinking gap.
- [Hard and soft systems thinking](concepts/hard-and-soft-systems-thinking.md) (Ch. 2): Checkland's distinction between problems defined well enough to model directly (hard) and problems whose definition is itself in dispute (soft).
- [Homeostasis](concepts/homeostasis.md) (Ch. 8): The special case of a balancing loop whose goal is simply "stay the same," like a thermostat holding a temperature.
- [Iceberg model](concepts/iceberg-model.md) (Ch. 5): A depth-of-analysis discipline with four bands: event (what happened), pattern (has it happened before), structure (what arrangement produces the pattern) and mental model (what belief made that structure seem reasonable).
- [Knowledge graph, RDF and SPARQL](concepts/knowledge-graph.md) (Ch. 23): A knowledge graph represents entities as nodes and typed relationships as labeled edges, a model of facts rather than dynamics; RDF is the W3C standard for writing it as subject-predicate-object triples, and SPARQL is its query language.
- [Leverage points](concepts/leverage-points.md) (Ch. 19): A ranked hierarchy of places to intervene in a system, collapsed to three bands (parameters, rules, goals and paradigms); higher bands move the structure rather than its current output, and are usually harder to reach.
- [Morris screening](concepts/morris-screening.md) (Ch. 18): A cheaper, coarser alternative to Sobol analysis for slow models, which ranks parameters by importance and flags those whose effect depends on others, without putting a number on each share.
- [Numerical integration method and time step](concepts/numerical-integration.md) (Ch. 17): How simulation software steps a model through time; Euler and Runge-Kutta methods can give meaningfully different output on the same model, so the method and time step are modeling decisions, not defaults to ignore.
- [Oscillation and overshoot](concepts/oscillation-and-overshoot.md) (Ch. 9): What delayed feedback produces: a balancing loop correcting for where things were overshoots its goal on every swing (oscillation), and a reinforcing loop compounds well past its limit before the stopping signal arrives (overshoot).
- [Property graph](concepts/property-graph.md) (Ch. 23): A graph data model where nodes and edges carry arbitrary key-value properties directly, rather than everything decomposed into triples; Neo4j's native model, queried in Cypher, not SPARQL.
- [Reduction (reductionism)](concepts/reduction.md) (Ch. 1): Cutting a problem down to the one part that looks broken and analyzing or fixing that part in isolation, discarding its interconnections.
- [Reference point (goal)](concepts/reference-point.md) (Ch. 8): The value a balancing loop steers toward; it comes from outside the loop and can be explicit or implicit, fixed or moving.
- [Reinforcing loop](concepts/reinforcing-loop.md) (Ch. 6): A feedback loop with an even number of negative links, which amplifies whatever is currently happening, compounding growth or decline.
- [Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)](concepts/retrieval-augmented-generation.md) (Ch. 23): An AI system fetching facts from a source before answering, rather than answering from what it learned in training; a knowledge graph is one structured source for it.
- [Sensitivity analysis](concepts/sensitivity-analysis.md) (Ch. 18): The discipline of finding out which of a model's guessed parameters its conclusions depend on, whose real payoff is a prioritized list of what to measure before trusting a specific recommendation.
- [Snowballing (exponential compounding)](concepts/snowballing.md) (Ch. 7): A reinforcing loop watched over several passes, where a constant multiplier on a growing base looks flat for a long stretch and then suddenly steep.
- [Sobol analysis (S1, ST, S2)](concepts/sobol-analysis.md) (Ch. 18): A global sensitivity analysis that varies every uncertain parameter at once across its range, runs the model on thousands of combinations, and divides the variance of the result among the parameters, alone (S1), in total (ST) and in pairs (S2).
- [Soft Systems Methodology (SSM)](concepts/soft-systems-methodology.md) (Ch. 2): Peter Checkland's method for problems whose definition is contested: surface every stakeholder's worldview, often in a rich picture, before building any formal model.
- [Source and sink (cloud)](concepts/source-and-sink.md) (Ch. 16): The cloud symbol at the open end of a pipe in a stock-and-flow diagram, marking where material comes from or goes to outside the model's boundary, untracked.
- [Specification gaming](concepts/specification-gaming.md) (Ch. 20): An AI system (or any optimizer) optimizing a measured proxy at the expense of the real goal it stood in for; once the neglected goal's damage reaches the metric, its structure is identical to Fixes That Fail.
- [Stock](concepts/stock.md) (Ch. 4): An accumulation: a quantity measurable at a single instant that changes only because something fills or drains it.
- [Stock-and-flow diagram and flow equations](concepts/stock-and-flow-diagram.md) (Ch. 16): The quantified version of a system sketch or translated CLD, with units and a starting value on every stock and an equation on every flow, stepped forward as a first-order difference equation.
- [Structural and behavioral validity](concepts/structural-and-behavioral-validity.md) (Ch. 18): Structural validity asks whether each mechanism in a model corresponds to something real, checked link by link without looking at output; behavioral validity asks whether the output reproduces observed history; passing one never implies passing the other.
- [System](concepts/system.md) (Ch. 1): A set of elements and the interconnections between them, organized around a function or purpose that the arrangement produces whether or not anyone designed it to.
- [System boundary](concepts/system-boundary.md) (Ch. 4): The dividing line between what a model treats as internal structure to be explained and what it treats as external input to be accepted as given; a decision, not a discovery.
- [System dynamics](concepts/system-dynamics.md) (Ch. 2): Jay Forrester's field of quantified models built from stocks, flows and feedback, simulated forward to see what behavior a structure produces over time.
- [Systems archetype](concepts/systems-archetype.md) (Ch. 11): A recurring causal loop shape, recognizable from its structure alone, independent of which domain is running through it.
- [Systems thinking](concepts/systems-thinking.md) (Ch. 1): A learnable discipline for explaining behavior that no single part explains, by drawing the structure of a whole system (its stocks, flows, feedback and boundary) and tracing what that structure produces over time.
- [Tame, messy and wicked problems](concepts/tame-messy-wicked-problems.md) (Ch. 21): An older vocabulary for problem difficulty: tame (clear formulation, right-or-wrong solution), wicked (no agreed formulation, solutions only better or worse, and attempting one changes the problem), and a mess (a system of interacting problems none solvable alone).
- [The checking loop and its sensor](concepts/checking-loop.md) (Ch. 24): Checking work is a balancing loop whose sensor is the check itself, so it comes to rest when the check finds nothing, whether or not the work is right; errors outside the check's boundary or kind accumulate undrained.
- [The trust-and-scrutiny loop](concepts/ai-trust-loop.md) (Ch. 20): Two reinforcing loops share an AI tool's accumulated trust; usage builds trust and trust drives usage, while rising trust lowers scrutiny per output, fewer errors are caught, and trust rises further, with the second loop's output an invisible absence.

## Procedures (21)

- [Audit and break an AI trust-and-scrutiny loop](procedures/break-the-trust-loop.md) (Ch. 20): Draw the AI-assisted workflow with an explicit "scrutiny per output" node, trace whether rising trust lowers it, find where you'd notice scrutiny falling to zero, then tie scrutiny to something other than trust.
- [Audit your checks for boundary and kind](procedures/audit-your-checks.md) (Ch. 24): List every check a piece of work has been through, write each one's boundary (what it looked at) and kind (what it could notice), find something none of them could catch, and add a check of a different kind rather than a wider version of an old one.
- [Build a causal loop diagram from a story](procedures/build-cld-from-story.md) (Ch. 10): Five steps in order: list the quantities, add polarity-marked arrows one at a time with any delays, check the chain closes, trace and label each loop, then read the whole loop aloud as a sentence a domain expert would nod along to.
- [CATWOE](procedures/catwoe.md) (Ch. 4): Checkland's six-question check of a drawn boundary (Customers, Actors, Transformation, Worldview, Owner, Environmental constraints) to surface the assumptions it hides.
- [Choose spreadsheet, simulation software, or PySD](procedures/choose-simulation-tooling.md) (Ch. 17): Before opening any software, draw a three-branch decision tree (stay in the spreadsheet, move to dedicated software, go straight to PySD) and write the specific trigger that would justify each.
- [Classify a growth stall](procedures/classify-a-growth-stall.md) (Ch. 13): When growth slows, decide whether it is one limit outside the system's own capacity (Limits to Growth), the system's own capacity, which the organization keeps declining to expand (Growth and Underinvestment), several limits at once (Attractiveness Principle), or a shared resource being drawn down (Tragedy of the Commons).
- [Damp an oscillation caused by delay](procedures/damp-an-oscillation.md) (Ch. 9): When a balancing loop overshoots because its feedback is late, shorten the delay, correct only part of the gap at a time, or widen the acceptable band; never react harder to the same late signal.
- [Diagnose which archetype is at work](procedures/diagnose-an-archetype.md) (Ch. 11): Start from the recurring behavior, form an archetype hypothesis with the diagnostic questions, then confirm it by drawing and tracing the actual links; a familiar story is a hint, not a diagnosis.
- [Draw a system boundary on purpose](procedures/draw-a-boundary.md) (Ch. 4): Choose the smallest slice of the world that still contains the structure producing the behavior in question, sketch its stocks and flows, and write a defense for every exclusion.
- [Find the unwritten goal a balancing loop defends](procedures/find-the-unwritten-goal.md) (Ch. 8): Sketch the loop's state, goal, gap and correction, then ask what number the goal holds and who set it; "nobody knows, we just feel it" is a valid and important result.
- [Hand-iterate a stock-and-flow model](procedures/hand-iterate-a-model.md) (Ch. 16): Give each stock a starting value and each flow an honest-guess equation, step the model forward one time step at a time in a table, read off which constraint binds, then shock it to see whether its loops fire.
- [Live-build a model with its audience](procedures/live-build-technique.md) (Ch. 25): Start from one shared, undisputed event, build the loop live one link at a time asking "does this match what you've seen?", and name the archetype only after the room has said something close to it in their own words.
- [Map the agents in a system as a graph](procedures/map-the-agents.md) (Ch. 23): Record each AI agent as triples (what it acts on, whose output it reads, who reviews it), then query for agents nobody reviews and for everything downstream of a given agent, as the list of what to trace before any CLD or simulation.
- [One-at-a-time swing test](procedures/one-at-a-time-swing-test.md) (Ch. 18): Move each of the shakiest guessed parameters 25% up and 25% down while holding the others at their guesses, rerun, and see whether the recommendation flips; draw the results as a tornado chart.
- [Put a model on trial](procedures/validate-a-model.md) (Ch. 18): Before acting on a model, test its structure link by link, test its behavior against history separately, then find which guesses its advice depends on, while running a counter-practice for each of three named biases.
- [Rank interventions on the leverage ladder](procedures/rank-interventions.md) (Ch. 19): For a diagnosed structure, list one intervention at each band (a parameter, a rule, a goal or mental model), mark the link or loop each touches, rate each on power and on reach separately, and label each as a real fix or a time-buying stopgap.
- [Run the iceberg on an incident](procedures/iceberg-analysis.md) (Ch. 5): Write one honest sentence for each of the iceberg's four bands (event, pattern, structure, mental model) before accepting any fix.
- [Trace a change three orders deep](procedures/trace-three-orders.md) (Ch. 22): For a real change (often an AI-augmented workflow), write the first-order effect, what it causes months later, and what that might cause a year out (labeled speculative), check each against the archetypes, and pick remedies that keep the first-order gain.
- [Trace and count a loop's polarity](procedures/trace-loop-polarity.md) (Ch. 6): Mark every link + or -, trace the loop once and count the minus signs; even (including zero) is reinforcing, odd is balancing.
- [Translate a CLD into stocks and flows](procedures/translate-cld-to-stock-and-flow.md) (Ch. 16): Six steps: snapshot-test every node, give each stock its pipes, turn every delay into a stock, make everything else an auxiliary, check every loop passes through a stock, then write and check each equation's units and polarity.
- [Use Cynefin to decide what to delegate to an AI agent](procedures/cynefin-as-delegation-guide.md) (Ch. 21): Classify a decision's Cynefin domain before any agent sees it, and let the domain, never the agent's fluency or track record, set the agent's job: a rule it calls, an analysis someone checks, probes it proposes, or a runbook it follows.

## Archetypes (11)

- [Accidental Adversaries](archetypes/accidental-adversaries.md) (Ch. 15): Two natural allies, each succeeding when the other does, each fix their own local gap in a way that obstructs the other, and those side effects close a vicious reinforcing loop that overpowers the partnership.
- [Attractiveness Principle](archetypes/attractiveness-principle.md) (Ch. 14): Limits to Growth with several different limits at once, each biting at a different size; relieving one only lets growth run until the next binds, so the real decision is which limits to relieve and which kinds of unattractiveness to accept on purpose.
- [Drift to Low Performance](archetypes/drift-to-low-performance.md) (Ch. 15): A goal quietly erodes toward whatever performance is being achieved, because lowering the target closes the gap faster than improving performance does.
- [Escalation](archetypes/escalation.md) (Ch. 12): Two actors each respond to the other's relative gain by increasing their own effort, so two balancing loops lock together into a reinforcing spiral toward an outcome neither wants.
- [Fixes That Fail](archetypes/fixes-that-fail.md) (Ch. 11): A quick fix relieves a symptom genuinely and immediately, but has a delayed side effect that makes the underlying problem worse, often triggering the same fix again, harder.
- [Growth and Underinvestment](archetypes/growth-and-underinvestment.md) (Ch. 14): Growth runs into a capacity limit that investment could remove, but investment is deferred, and the demand lost to poor performance is read as proof that demand never justified investing, while the performance standard quietly erodes.
- [Limits to Growth](archetypes/limits-to-growth.md) (Ch. 13): A reinforcing growth loop runs into a balancing loop generated by a real constraint (its carrying capacity), so growth slows and levels off, or declines if the constraint itself erodes, without anything having broken.
- [Shifting the Burden](archetypes/shifting-the-burden.md) (Ch. 11): A fast symptomatic fix and a slower fundamental fix compete to close the same gap, and reliance on the symptomatic fix erodes the capability to apply the fundamental one, so the quick fix keeps winning.
- [Shifting the Burden to the Intervenor](archetypes/shifting-the-burden-to-the-intervenor.md) (Ch. 11): Shifting the Burden where the symptomatic fix is a specific outside fixer available on demand (a consultant, the one expert, an AI agent), so internal capability erodes faster than in the ordinary case.
- [Success to the Successful](archetypes/success-to-the-successful.md) (Ch. 15): Two competitors draw from one limited resource, and an early edge for one, however it arose, is reinforced by the allocation rule itself, starving the other of what it would need to catch up regardless of merit.
- [Tragedy of the Commons](archetypes/tragedy-of-the-commons.md) (Ch. 13): Several independent actors share a finite, unpriced resource; each is individually incentivized to take more than a sustainable share because holding back only cedes it to others, so the resource degrades for everyone.

## Heuristics (41)

- [A calm base case doesn't prove a loop harmless](heuristics/calm-base-case-proves-nothing.md) (Ch. 16): If a loop never fires under baseline conditions, shock the model and compare runs with the loop intact and switched off before concluding it doesn't matter.
- [A commons needs rules its users can see kept](heuristics/commons-need-collective-rules.md) (Ch. 13): Over-use of a shared resource can't be fixed by one user's restraint; it takes rules every user agrees to, can see being kept and can enforce, whether set by a regulator or by the users themselves.
- [A consistent diagram isn't a true one](heuristics/consistent-is-not-true.md) (Ch. 10): A causal diagram can pass every notation check and still tell a story nobody who works in the system believes, so say it aloud to them before building on it.
- [A correct trace isn't a correct archetype](heuristics/a-trace-is-not-a-definition.md) (Ch. 24): A diagram can close and trace to the polarity claimed and still not be the archetype it's named after; tracing confirms polarity, and only the literature confirms the structure.
- [A good historical fit is easy to manufacture](heuristics/fit-is-easy-to-manufacture.md) (Ch. 18): Any model with guessed parameters can be tuned to reproduce the past, so a good fit proves only that the model can be tuned, not that its mechanisms are right.
- [A plus sign into a stock means "adds to"](heuristics/plus-into-a-stock.md) (Ch. 10): On an arrow from a flow into a stock, + means the flow adds to the stock, not that the two move together; a stock keeps rising while its net inflow is positive, even as that inflow shrinks.
- [Act while the curve still looks flat](heuristics/exponential-looks-flat.md) (Ch. 7): If you have traced a reinforcing loop, decide on intervention while its output still looks boring, because compounding is invisible to the eye until several rounds have run.
- [Anchor goals outside the system's own results](heuristics/anchor-goals-outside.md) (Ch. 15): Tie a target to something the system's recent performance can't move (customer need, a competitor's level, your own best year), so misses stay gaps instead of becoming the new target.
- [Ask what a fix does a few rounds out](heuristics/ask-what-the-fix-does-later.md) (Ch. 11): Before reaching for a fix that works now, ask what it will do after it has been applied several times, because a fix that works now and fails later gets reached for again.
- [Being right is necessary, never sufficient](heuristics/right-is-not-sufficient.md) (Ch. 2): A correct model that nobody believes has changed nothing, so plan for how the audience will come to accept a model as early as you plan the model itself.
- [Bound interacting agents and watch them together](heuristics/bound-interacting-agents.md) (Ch. 21): When independently built agents react to one another's outputs with nobody coordinating, give each hard limits it can't reason past and monitor their joint behavior, because what they do together belongs to none of them.
- [Choose the model by the question](heuristics/choose-model-by-question.md) (Ch. 23): Three questions, three models: what structure is producing this? (a CLD); how will it behave over time? (a stock-and-flow simulation); what do we know and what is connected to what? (a knowledge graph).
- [Deterministic-gate principle](heuristics/deterministic-gate-principle.md) (Ch. 21): If a decision should produce the identical answer every time given identical inputs, it belongs behind a rule-based gate, not in open-ended judgment by a person or an agent.
- [Diffuse resistance means an unnamed balancing loop](heuristics/change-initiative-meets-balancing-loop.md) (Ch. 8): When a change effort meets small, reasonable resistance from every direction and nobody is visibly opposing it, look for a balancing loop defending a goal the change never addressed.
- [Don't blame people for oscillation](heuristics/oscillation-is-structural.md) (Ch. 9): Repeated overshoot under delayed feedback is the expected behavior of the structure, so look for the delay before looking for the person who "should have acted sooner."
- [Don't react harder to a late signal](heuristics/dont-react-harder-to-late-signals.md) (Ch. 9): If a system oscillates because its feedback is delayed, reacting faster or more forcefully to the same late signal enlarges the swings.
- [End an escalation by changing what's compared](heuristics/change-the-comparison.md) (Ch. 12): When two parties are locked in a relative-position race, change the structure of the comparison (a verifiable shared limit, a new dimension to compete on) rather than asking one side to stop first.
- [Fluency is not system-level correctness](heuristics/fluency-is-not-correctness.md) (Ch. 1): A fast, confident, internally consistent answer to the question asked says nothing about whether the question was the right one, so check the question, not the polish.
- [Graphs, not just chats](heuristics/graphs-not-just-chats.md) (Ch. 23): When an AI agent answers factual questions about a system, give it a knowledge graph to query so its answers are reproducible, traceable to sources and shared, rather than left in one conversation.
- [Growth isn't proof of a reinforcing loop](heuristics/growing-is-not-reinforcing.md) (Ch. 7): Before crediting a growth spiral to a loop, check that each round's output is what drives the next round's input, not an outside event moving the same way.
- [Keep the capability exercised on purpose](heuristics/exercise-the-capability.md) (Ch. 20): When a tool or agent reliably does a skilled task, schedule people to do it without the tool and compare their answer with the tool's, so the human capability doesn't quietly atrophy.
- [Know which reduction you just made](heuristics/know-which-reduction-you-made.md) (Ch. 26): Every model, archetype, boundary and summary is a reduction; the discipline isn't avoiding reduction but knowing which one you made, drawing what it left out, and watching what pushes back.
- [Label a stopgap as a stopgap](heuristics/label-the-stopgap.md) (Ch. 19): A low-leverage fix is fine as a deliberate bridge while a structural fix is built, as long as everyone knows which job it's doing and what it's buying time for.
- [Moving to software is an escalation of commitment](heuristics/software-is-escalation-of-commitment.md) (Ch. 17): Graduating a model to simulation software or code buys precision and shareability but costs a learning curve, a model fewer people can read or argue with, and a new way to be wrong (mistaking "it runs" for "it's correct").
- [Past performance may be a record of past allocation](heuristics/past-performance-may-be-allocation.md) (Ch. 15): When resources go to whoever performed best last round, part of each party's track record was produced by the allocation rule itself, not by merit.
- [Pressure keeps analysis at the event level](heuristics/pressure-shrinks-the-iceberg.md) (Ch. 5): The pull to react to the event and move on is strongest when stakes are highest, so the deeper bands need to be asked for deliberately under pressure.
- [Quantify early to surface disagreement, not for precision](heuristics/quantify-to-surface-disagreement.md) (Ch. 16): Writing an explicit flow equation, even with guessed numbers, forces disagreements that a qualitative diagram lets stay vague, and those are cheapest to resolve on paper.
- [Reach for a knowledge graph only when facts outgrow the diagram](heuristics/reach-for-a-graph-when.md) (Ch. 23): A well-drawn CLD or stock-and-flow diagram is the better tool for most systems thinking; a knowledge graph earns its cost when the facts are too many, change too often, must join external data, or must come back the same every time an agent answers.
- [Recurring, differently-caused failures point at structure](heuristics/recurring-causes-mean-structure.md) (Ch. 1): If each incident has a different, correctly fixed root cause but the overall metric doesn't improve, the cause is in the structure connecting the parts, not in any part.
- [Reinforcing isn't good, balancing isn't bad](heuristics/polarity-carries-no-value.md) (Ch. 6): Loop type says how a loop behaves, not whether that behavior is welcome; judge each loop by the direction it is running and the goal it defends.
- [Self-reported confidence is not evidence](heuristics/self-reported-confidence.md) (Ch. 20): A confidence signal generated by the system being trusted (a badge, a score, an assertion of certainty) tells you how the output is phrased, not whether it is right or addresses the right question.
- [Snapshot test](heuristics/snapshot-test.md) (Ch. 16): To tell a stock from a flow, imagine stopping time and photographing the system; whatever you could still count or measure in the photograph is a stock, and whatever only makes sense as a rate is a flow.
- [Suspect a boundary that matches your data](heuristics/convenience-boundary-trap.md) (Ch. 4): If a model's boundary sits exactly where the easy data ends, it was probably drawn for convenience rather than for the question.
- [Swarm test](heuristics/swarm-test.md) (Ch. 3): Ask "is anyone in charge?"; if coordination is assigned by someone accountable, use the ordinary stock/flow and feedback toolkit; if it emerges from many actors following local rules with nobody in charge, treat it as a swarm.
- [Unrun code is a citation nobody looked up](heuristics/unrun-code-is-an-unchecked-citation.md) (Ch. 24): Plausible-looking output (a citation, a code example, printed results) that was generated rather than verified looks exactly like the real thing, and only running or looking it up catches it.
- [Verify after every translation](heuristics/verify-after-translation.md) (Ch. 17): After moving a model between tools, formats or languages (an XMILE round trip, a PySD import, a port to code), reproduce a known run on identical inputs before trusting it.
- [When a stock is changing, name the flow](heuristics/name-the-flow.md) (Ch. 4): A sentence like "our headcount is dropping" hides which flow is responsible, and the inflow and outflow readings call for different fixes.
- [When both sides blame the other, look for the whole loop](heuristics/both-sides-blame-the-other.md) (Ch. 15): If two parties who should be allies each call the other "the problem" and each can accurately describe its own behavior as a reasonable response, suspect Accidental Adversaries.
- [When growth stalls, look at the limit, not the engine](heuristics/push-on-the-limit.md) (Ch. 13): Slowing growth against a real constraint is the system behaving as designed; pushing harder on the growth engine mostly makes the constraint push back harder.
- [When something gets past a check, fix the check](heuristics/every-check-has-a-boundary-and-a-kind.md) (Ch. 24): When an error slips past a check, ask whether it fell outside the check's boundary (where it looked) or outside its kind (what it could notice), and change the check, not just the bug.
- [Write standards specific enough to fail](heuristics/standards-specific-enough-to-fail.md) (Ch. 24): A check can only catch what its standard is specific enough to name, so a vague description ("a 2x2-plus-center diagram") will pass every wrong arrangement.
