Feedback in the technical sense needs no conversation and no person, only a loop. However many links it has, every closed loop is exactly one of two types:

- **Reinforcing** (R): amplifies whatever is happening. More leads, through the chain, to more.
- **Balancing** (B): resists change, pulling the system back toward a reference point.

Never both, never neither, never in between. The type is determined by tracing polarity around the loop and counting minus signs (even is R, odd is B), not by how the loop feels.

## Loop dominance

Real systems contain several loops, often sharing links. Which loop *dominates* depends on where the system stands. A recruitment system can have a reinforcing reputation loop (success, reputation, applicants, success) and a balancing training-capacity loop (applicants, headcount, less training per person, worse execution, less success) sharing a stretch of path. While the workforce is small the spiral rules; once headcount outgrows the training staff the brake takes over. Shared path segments are normal, not a sign of a drawing error.
