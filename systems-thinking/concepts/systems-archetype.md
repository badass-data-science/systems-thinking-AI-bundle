Learn the shape once and it shows up in a supply chain, a hiring pipeline and a code-review process alike. The book teaches ten: Fixes That Fail, Shifting the Burden (with its intervenor variant), Escalation, Tragedy of the Commons, Limits to Growth, Growth and Underinvestment, the Attractiveness Principle, Success to the Successful, Drift to Low Performance and Accidental Adversaries.

Each is taught in the same order, which is also the right order for using one: the story and the behavior you'd notice first; the structure, confirmed by tracing (a familiar story is a hint, not a diagnosis); then where the leverage lies.

An archetype is a hypothesis to test against the actual links, not a label to announce. When communicating, ask the archetype's diagnostic question and let the audience recognize the shape before naming it (the live-build technique).
