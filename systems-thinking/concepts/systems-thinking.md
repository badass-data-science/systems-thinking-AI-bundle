Systems thinking asks "what does the structure of this system keep producing, whichever part happens to break?" rather than "what broke this time?" A system that generates a new proximate cause every period isn't unlucky each time; it behaves as its structure predicts, and the structure hasn't been examined.

It is a defined discipline with a literature going back to the 1940s, specific tools, and habits that take practice. It is not a personality trait or a synonym for "big-picture thinking." The practice is concrete: draw the structure on paper and trace what it produces.

This book's version borrows tools from several traditions rather than teaching any one school whole: stocks and flows and causal loop diagrams from system dynamics, CATWOE from Soft Systems Methodology, and the elements/interconnections/purpose vocabulary from general systems theory by way of Donella Meadows.

## Why it matters more with AI agents

Two reasons, kept separate. First, speed: when fixes are cheap and instant there is no natural brake forcing anyone to ask the structural question, so the "diagnose, fix, declare victory" cycle can repeat at machine pace. Second, fluency: an agent's confident, correct, well-formatted answer to the question it was asked says nothing about whether the question was the structural one. As agents absorb part-level analysis, the scarce human skill shifts toward recognizing when the whole is what's broken.
