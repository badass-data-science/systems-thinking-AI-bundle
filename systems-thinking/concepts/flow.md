Examples: units mined per week, people hired per month, tickets closed per day. A flow can jump to a new value the instant whatever governs it changes; only the stock it feeds carries the accumulated history.

## Why the distinction matters

Plain English blurs stocks and flows constantly. "Our headcount is dropping" sounds like a statement about a stock but is a claim about the *net* flow (hiring minus attrition), and it fits two different diagnoses with two different fixes: people are leaving too fast (reduce attrition) or we never hired enough (increase recruitment). Precision about which flow is responsible is the difference between fixing the problem and fixing the wrong half of the sentence.
