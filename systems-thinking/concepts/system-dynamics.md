Forrester, at MIT, took the feedback thinking cybernetics had formalized for engineered control and applied it to organizations, economies and eventually the planet. A system dynamics model states the stocks (what accumulates), the flows (rates that fill and drain them) and the feedback between them, with real units and equations, and simulates forward. The point is to see the behavior the structure produces regardless of any single decision.

## Control question versus structure question

Cybernetics and system dynamics both draw loops, but they ask different questions. Control theory asks *how do I keep this on target?*, which presupposes a setpoint and an error signal. System dynamics asks *why does this structure produce this behavior over time?*, which presupposes no target at all. A system that faithfully produces the behavior its unexamined structure generates isn't missing a setpoint, and tightening a control loop won't fix it; the structure needs redrawing.
