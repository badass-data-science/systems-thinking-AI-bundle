An ant isn't smart; a colony with no ant in charge still finds the shortest path to food. Complexity science formalizes how coordination emerges from local rules with no central controller. Kevin Kelly argued in 1994 that human-built systems were heading the same way.

Its challenge to the structure-drawing traditions: a lot of coordinated behavior comes from no drawn structure at all, only from many actors each following local rules. The book treats the swarm idea as a lens (see the swarm test) rather than teaching complexity science's toolkit, which is out of scope.
