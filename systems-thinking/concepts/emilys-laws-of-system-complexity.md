**First Law:** *For every reduction, there is a greater and opposite clusterfuck.*

**Second Law:** *The first law is a reductionist statement.*

The First Law misquotes Newton's third law on purpose, and the working word is *greater*. A reduction keeps the part and discards the interconnections. Those interconnections keep operating and push back on their own schedule, through structure the fix never looked at. The reaction can outweigh the action because of delays and reinforcing loops the reduction never drew; the Fixes That Fail archetype (Ch. 11) is the structural mechanism.

The Second Law is the honesty clause. A one-line law about complexity is itself a reduction, so treat the First Law as a warning label, not a law of nature.

## How to use it

Before shipping a fix to one part, ask what the fix's interconnections will do in response, and when. If you can't name any, you haven't looked yet.
