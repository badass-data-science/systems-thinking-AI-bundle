Every model has a boundary, whether or not anyone drew it on purpose; an unchosen boundary is usually the laziest one available. What's inside is something you claim to understand; what's outside is taken as given.

There's no universally correct boundary, only a chosen one, and everything downstream (every diagram, model and fix) depends on whether it was chosen deliberately. The most common mistake is drawing it *conveniently*, around whatever data already exists, rather than around what the question requires. A narrow boundary gives a model that's clean, fast to present, and wrong the next time something outside it (a border closure, a supplier's ownership) moves.

A boundary also decides what counts as inside each stage, not only where the chain starts and stops. A flow that leaves the system through a pipe nobody drew (material quietly diverted to another use) makes the model keep counting material that will never arrive.

Re-arguing whether something belongs inside the box already drawn is a different exercise from redrawing the box. Know which one a meeting is doing.
