A system has three parts:

- **Elements**: the things in it (people, materials, machines, services, accounts).
- **Interconnections**: the relationships and flows between the elements (who reports to whom, what triggers what, what moves where).
- **Function or purpose**: what the arrangement produces over time. It may differ from what anyone intended.

The definition puts interconnections ahead of inventory. Change the elements and you often still have the same system, producing the same behavior. Change the interconnections and you usually have a different system, even if every element stays the same.

The definition says nothing about which part is currently broken. That's deliberate: a system that keeps failing while its broken parts keep changing is telling you the cause lives in an interconnection that didn't change.

## How to use it

When an analysis names only elements ("the supplier," "the staffing level"), ask which interconnection carried the problem from one element to the next, and what the whole arrangement keeps producing regardless of which element is under strain. That last question, the *purpose* question, exists only at the level of the whole. It's the one a part-level analysis, human or AI, doesn't ask unless someone asks it.
