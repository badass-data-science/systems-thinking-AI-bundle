- **First order**: the immediate, visible effect of a change.
- **Second order**: what that effect causes once it has had time to play out. Often delayed, and often looking structurally disconnected from the original cause.
- **Third order**: what the second-order effect causes in turn; further out and explicitly more speculative.

Stop where the chain gets too thin to defend with a straight face. Asking "and then what?" indefinitely collapses into unfalsifiable speculation, as useless as never asking.

Second- and third-order effects frequently turn out to be known archetypes; naming the archetype supplies the remedy.

**Vocabulary warning**: "order" here (distance along a causal chain) is unrelated to a "first-order difference equation" (how many previous steps an update depends on) and to Sobol's "first-order index" S1 (variance explained by one parameter alone).
