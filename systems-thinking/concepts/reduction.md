Reduction is the core move of analytical thinking: isolate the component that is misbehaving, explain it, fix it. It is the right move for understanding a part. It is the wrong move for understanding why a whole keeps failing, because the reduction throws away the interconnections, and the interconnections keep operating whether or not anyone drew them.

A reduction is not a mistake in itself. Each fix in a chain of reductions can be correct. The failure is stopping at the reduction when the evidence (recurring failures with a different proximate cause each time) says the structure, not any one part, is producing the problem.
