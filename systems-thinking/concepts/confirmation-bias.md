title: Confirmation bias
summary: The pull toward evidence, and toward modeling choices, that confirm what you already believed.
chapters: [18]
attribution: Named bias in the judgment and decision-making literature; see Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)
links:
  related: [structural-and-behavioral-validity, validate-a-model]

---

In modeling it shows up as tuning: adjusting guesses or adding links until the output matches what you suspected. **Counter-practice**: check structure separately, link by link, ideally with someone who didn't build the model; and write down before each run what result would prove you wrong.

Not a moral failing. It's how judgment works under uncertainty, so the defense is a named bias with a concrete counter-practice, not a resolution to be more objective.
