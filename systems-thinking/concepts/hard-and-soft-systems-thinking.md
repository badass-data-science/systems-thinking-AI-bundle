**Hard** systems thinking fits problems whose goal and boundaries are agreed, so you can build a model and reason over it. **Soft** systems thinking fits problems where stakeholders disagree about what the problem is; there, surfacing worldviews has to come before any formal model can mean anything.

The tame/wicked distinction (Rittel and Webber) is close to this one, not to Cynefin: both sort by whether the problem's own definition is settled.

Practical test: if two stakeholders would write different problem statements, you're in soft territory, and a technically excellent hard model will be argued with rather than acted on.
