- **Tame** (Rittel and Webber): a clear formulation, a definite stopping point, a solution that is right or wrong.
- **Wicked** (Rittel and Webber; Rittel used the term in seminars from the 1960s): no agreed formulation, no natural stopping point, solutions only better or worse and judged after the fact, because attempting one changes the problem.
- **Mess** (usually traced to Ackoff): not one problem but a system of interacting problems, none solvable in isolation.

## Translation table

The straight mapping (tame = clear or complicated; wicked = complex or chaotic) is close enough to be useful and wrong enough to be dangerous. Cynefin sorts by the *stability of cause and effect*; tame/wicked sorts by whether the *problem's definition* is settled. They correlate but differ. Wicked corresponds most closely to Checkland's *soft* systems (the definition itself is contested); tame to *hard*. A mess is what CATWOE interrogates: a boundary drawn around what looks like one problem but is several tangled together.
