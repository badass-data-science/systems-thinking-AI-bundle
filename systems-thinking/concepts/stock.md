Examples: inventory in a warehouse, staff on payroll, material in transit, a backlog of tickets, accumulated trust, a reputation. A stock has inertia: it never changes on its own, only through its inflows and outflows. That inertia is what makes stocks the memory of a system and the source of its delays.

Stocks also usually set the flows. A low inventory hurries the next order; a swelling headcount thins out training. A stock reaching back to change the flows that fill or drain it is what feedback is.

Sterman's snapshot test tells a stock from a flow: freeze time, and whatever you could still count is a stock.
