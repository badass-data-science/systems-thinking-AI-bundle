**Oscillation**: a balancing loop acting on late feedback ends up correcting for the past state, not the current one. It overshoots, corrects, overshoots again. Activity ramps up; the signal arrives late and all at once; the correction is sharp because catching up needs a big swing; on the way down, the material delay keeps the state falling after the correction, and the information delay keeps the correction in place too long. Then the cycle restarts, because the structure never changed.

**Overshoot** of a reinforcing loop: a hiring spiral keeps filling the bench while the training shortfall takes weeks to show up as poor execution. By the time the brake bites, headcount is well past what the trainers can sustain, and it bites late and hard. The delay turns the meeting of a spiral and its limit into an overshoot and fall.

## Oscillation is not anyone's fault

It is the mathematically expected result of acting on delayed feedback, even when every decision was reasonable given what was known. Blaming whoever "should have acted sooner" diagnoses a structural problem as a personal one.
