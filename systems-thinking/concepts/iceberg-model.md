Each band sits beneath the last and is harder to see and more durable to fix:

1. **Event**: a single occurrence. Most postmortems stop here.
2. **Pattern**: the same kind of event across time, visible only when several are lined up. A pattern is a different kind of fact from an event, not a bigger one.
3. **Structure**: the arrangement of stocks, flows and feedback that produces the pattern, whichever event triggers it this time.
4. **Mental model**: the belief held by the people running the system that made the structure seem reasonable to build or leave in place.

The durable fix rarely lives in the event band. The mental-model band connects to the highest leverage points (goals and paradigms, Ch. 19).

## Two cautions

The iceberg gets shallower exactly when you need it deepest: under pressure (an incident in progress, a meeting in an hour) the pull is to react to the event and move on.

Don't treat the mental model as the culprit. Beliefs at the base were usually reasonable given what people knew when they built the structure. The discipline is updating the belief once the evidence changes, not shaming whoever held it.
