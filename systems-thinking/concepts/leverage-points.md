- **Low leverage: numbers, parameters, subsidies.** What almost everyone reaches for first: visible, easy, needing no one's permission. (Raising overtime pay.)
- **Mid leverage: rules.** Incentive structures, resource-allocation formulas, who may change what. (Changing a budget rule so it no longer feeds the early winner.)
- **High leverage: goals and paradigms.** The goals the system is organized around and the mindset that makes them look reasonable; these move the structure itself. (Redefining "success" in quarterly reviews to count what each success costs.) This band corresponds to the iceberg's mental-model layer.

The value for a working professional is the *ordering*, not recall of all twelve of Meadows' rungs.

**Power and reach are different axes.** Theoretical power rises up the ladder while what you can do on Monday morning falls; a leverage analysis that names only the strongest lever hasn't finished. A low rung can also simply be right: in the book's sensitivity test a parameter (more capacity) beat a rule (no corner-cutting) at the model's guesses. The ladder says where power usually lies; only the model says which push wins here.

"Leverage" here is Meadows' sense (how much a place in the structure moves the whole), not the financial sense of borrowing.
