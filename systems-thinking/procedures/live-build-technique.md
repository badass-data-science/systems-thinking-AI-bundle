## When to use

Whenever a model's finding implicates the people who will hear it, which is almost always.

## Steps

1. Open with one shared, undisputed event or frustration, in the room's own words.
2. Add one link at a time on the whiteboard, phrased as a question ("so when a batch comes back bad, you catch it by hand, and that fixes it for this batch?").
3. After each addition, ask "does this match what you've seen?" Adjust if not.
4. Continue until the loop closes. Wait for the room to state the pattern itself.
5. Only then name the archetype, as a label for what they already said.
6. Treat every pushback as a link or boundary to defend or fix, not as resistance to overcome.
7. Note who has now been consulted, and who (often the party whose process the fundamental fix would change) comes next.

## Checks

- Nobody in the room heard the archetype's name before describing the pattern.
- The loop on the board uses the room's words.
- No node or link names a person or group as the cause.

## Common mistakes

- Leading with the archetype's name and a finished diagram. It invites the room to argue with the *label* ("we are NOT shifting the burden") rather than the substance, and lands as an accusation.
- Blame-shaped language ("mining is causing the problem"). Systems problems are ones where every actor's behavior looks reasonable from their own seat; communicate the loop, not a culprit inside it.
- Skipping the next room.
