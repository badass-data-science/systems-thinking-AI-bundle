## When to use

After diagnosing a structure, before choosing what to do about it.

## Steps

1. List at least one intervention at each band: a **parameter** to adjust, a **rule** to change, a **goal or mental model** to challenge.
2. For each, mark the specific link or loop on the diagram it changes. An intervention that touches no link isn't an intervention on this structure.
3. Rate **power**: how much would it move the structure, not just this period's output?
4. Rate **reach** separately: could you actually attempt it now, given the organization's real political and resource constraints?
5. If a model exists, test the leading candidates against each other in it rather than trusting the ladder's ordering alone.
6. Choose, and label the choice honestly: a real fix, or a stopgap buying time for a higher-band fix. If it's a stopgap, name the fix it's buying time for.

## Checks

- Every candidate maps to a link or loop.
- Power and reach were rated separately.
- Any stopgap names the fix it buys time for.

## Common mistakes

- Treating "high leverage" as "the right thing to do next."
- Banning parameter fixes on principle. A quick stabilization honestly deployed while a structural fix is built is defensible.
- Mistaking a stopgap for a fix (the Fixes That Fail trap).
