## When to use

After drawing a boundary and before trusting it, and again before presenting a model to anyone whose seat in the organization differs from yours.

## Steps

Answer each question about the system inside your boundary:

1. **Customers**: who is affected by this system, for better or worse?
2. **Actors**: who carries out the activities inside the boundary?
3. **Transformation**: what does the system convert, from what input to what output?
4. **Worldview**: what perspective has to be true for that transformation to look worthwhile at all?
5. **Owner**: who has the authority to change or shut down this system?
6. **Environmental constraints**: what sits outside the boundary, unchangeable from inside, that the system must still work within?

Then write down any answer that surprised you, and decide whether it changes the boundary or a goal.

## Checks

- The Worldview answer is a sentence someone could disagree with. If it's a truism, dig further.
- The Owner is a role with real authority, not the person who drew the diagram.

## Common mistakes

- Answering only the comfortable letters (Transformation, Actors). The Worldview usually does the surprising work.
- Running CATWOE before a boundary exists; it interrogates a boundary, it doesn't draw one.

## Worked example

For a production chain that converts raw material into a stockpile of finished units, the Transformation is clean, but the Worldview that makes "convert as fast as possible" look obviously worthwhile is a belief nobody had stated: that readiness matters more than quality. Nobody voted on it; it was baked into the order of the stages.
