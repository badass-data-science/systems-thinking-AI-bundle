## When to use

When something keeps returning to a level nobody chose, or a well-designed change "just can't get traction" against resistance from many directions with no visible saboteur.

## Steps

1. Name the **current state** being governed, as a quantity.
2. Name the **corrective action** you can see the system taking when the state moves.
3. Draw the loop: state → gap (`+`) → corrective action (`+`) → state (`−`, if the action reduces the state). Confirm one minus sign: balancing.
4. Draw the goal as a box *outside* the loop, feeding the gap. Ask: what number does this box hold today?
5. Interview yourself, or better someone who has lived through the system's rhythm: at what level does the correction start? What is "too high" or "too much"?
6. Ask who or what set that goal: a policy, a budget, a habit, last year's number, a comparison with someone else.
7. Decide: keep it, write it down, or change it. If a change effort moved the state without moving the goal, the fix is to change the goal explicitly.

## Checks

- The goal is outside the loop on the diagram and not counted in the polarity trace.
- You can say who or what set the goal, or you've recorded that nobody can.

## Common mistakes

- Treating "nobody knows, we just feel it" as a failed exercise. It is the exercise working: an unwritten goal has been surfaced.
- Assuming stability is good. A loop defending a bad implicit goal defeats needed change indefinitely.
- Fighting the symptom (each instance of resistance) instead of naming the goal.
