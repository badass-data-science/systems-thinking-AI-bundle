## When to use

A problem keeps recurring and you suspect a known shape. Also when someone has already announced an archetype and you need to check it.

## Steps

1. **Describe the behavior** you'd notice first, over time: what keeps happening, getting worse, stalling or diverging.
2. **Ask the diagnostic questions** and note every one that gets a yes:
   - Does the problem come back worse after each round of the same fix, as if the fix fed it? → Fixes That Fail
   - Is a symptomatic fix crowding out a fundamental one while the capability for the fundamental fix atrophies? → Shifting the Burden (an on-demand outside fixer, person or AI: the intervenor variant)
   - Are two parties responding to each other's relative gain rather than their own absolute welfare? → Escalation
   - Are several independent actors individually incentivized to over-consume one shared, finite resource? → Tragedy of the Commons
   - Has growth slowed against a limit while the response is to push harder on growth? → Limits to Growth
   - Is a decision not to invest being "confirmed" by the shortfall it causes? → Growth and Underinvestment
   - Is growth hitting several different limits at once, more than can be relieved? → Attractiveness Principle
   - Is an early, possibly arbitrary edge reinforced by the allocation rule itself? → Success to the Successful
   - Has the goal moved down to meet results? → Drift to Low Performance
   - Are two natural allies each fixing their own problem in a way that hurts the other? → Accidental Adversaries
3. **Draw the actual structure** for the leading hypothesis (or build it from the story) and compare it with the archetype's signature: the same loops, the same polarity on each, the delays in the same places.
4. **Trace every loop** and check the R/B labels match the archetype's.
5. **Discriminate between close neighbors** (see below). If two fit, look for the link that differs.
6. **Apply the archetype's leverage** to this case, in its terms.
7. If no archetype's structure matches the traced links, say so. Not every problem is an archetype.

## Checks

- The diagnosis rests on traced links, not on the story's resemblance to an archetype's story.
- Each archetype loop maps to a real, nameable link in the situation.
- The archetype's boundary includes only the parties and links the archetype holds.

## Common mistakes

Close neighbors and the question that separates them:

- Fixes That Fail vs Shifting the Burden: does the side effect worsen the *problem*, or erode the *capability* for a better fix?
- Escalation vs Limits to Growth: is there a real wall, or an unbounded comparison?
- Tragedy of the Commons vs Success to the Successful: is the shared resource drawn down by everyone, or allocated by a rule that feeds the leader?
- Limits to Growth vs Growth and Underinvestment vs Attractiveness Principle: one limit outside the system's own capacity, the system's own capacity repeatedly left unexpanded, or several limits at once?
- Accidental Adversaries vs Escalation: allies whose fixes hurt each other, or rivals responding to relative position?
- Drift to Low Performance vs a single miss: has the target been lowered repeatedly in response to misses?

Also: announcing the archetype's name before the audience has recognized the shape (see the live-build technique), and drawing every nearby problem into the archetype.
