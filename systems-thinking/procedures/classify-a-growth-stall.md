## When to use

Growth that used to compound has slowed, and someone is proposing either to find what broke or to push harder on growth.

## Steps

1. Confirm there is a reinforcing growth loop: is each round's output really driving the next round's input? (Rule out an outside tailwind.)
2. List every constraint that could be slowing it, each as a balancing loop. For each, ask what kind of limit it is (money, build time, a fixed external quantity, something no budget can touch).
3. Ask whether the constraint is shared with other actors drawing on the same resource. If several independent actors are depleting it, it's **Tragedy of the Commons**; the fix is collective.
4. Count the limits that bind now or will soon.
   - **One, outside the system's own capacity** (a market's size, a population, a regulation, a resource): **Limits to Growth**. The limit is often removable, and removing or relaxing it is the leverage; if it can't be moved, plan around it.
   - **One, and it is the system's own capacity**: check the history. Has the same decision not to invest been reconfirmed after each failure, using that failure as evidence? Then it's **Growth and Underinvestment**, the special case of Limits to Growth where the organization is declining to remove its own limit. Judge investment by potential demand, invest ahead given the build delay, and hold the performance standard fixed. If investment is already keeping pace with demand, it's plain Limits to Growth, and the investment is the relaxing.
   - **Several, more than the budget can relieve**: **Attractiveness Principle**. Choose which to relieve and which unattractiveness to accept on purpose.
5. Check delays on each limit's feedback. A delayed limit means overshoot and fall rather than smooth leveling, so watch its leading indicators.
6. Write the recommendation against the limit, never "push harder on the engine."

## Checks

- You named the constraint specifically, and said whether it is fixed, removable or shared.
- The recommendation doesn't rely on more effort on the growth engine.

## Common mistakes

- "We must have broken something" (hunting for a part to repair).
- Pushing harder on growth against a binding limit.
- Calling a self-imposed capacity limit a law of nature.
- Treating each new bottleneck in an Attractiveness structure as unrelated.
