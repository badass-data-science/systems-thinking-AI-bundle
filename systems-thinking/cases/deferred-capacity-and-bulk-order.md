## Situation

For three years an organization deferred building assembly capacity that takes most of a year to bring online, reasoning each time that demand was too choppy to justify it. Then the largest order in its history arrived. The line couldn't keep up; delivery failed.

## Analysis

At the next budget review the failure became evidence: "we told you demand wasn't reliable enough to build for." The argument won; the investment was deferred again, justified by the failure it caused. Lost orders lowered the felt need to invest (R2), and poor delivery quietly lowered what counted as acceptable (R3).

## Lesson

Tell: the same decision is reconfirmed after every failure using that failure as evidence. Judge investment by the demand the organization could have, invest ahead of demand given the build time, and hold the delivery standard fixed in writing. A sentence that would break the spiral: "We lost that order because of our capacity, not because the demand wasn't there."
