## Situation

Commissioned by the Club of Rome and built with Forrester's methods at MIT, the model treated global population, industrial output, pollution, food production and resource depletion as one interconnected system. Across a wide range of assumptions, its structure produced overshoot and collapse within the following century, because the feedback structure behaved that way once its constraints began to bind, not because any single input was pessimistic.

## Analysis

The critics largely didn't dispute the mathematics. They disputed where the system boundary was drawn, which feedback loops were included or omitted, and which parameters were real versus contestable assumptions. Those are value-laden choices, and contesting them is easier than accepting the conclusion.

## Lesson

Boundary and parameter choices are where a model will be attacked, so they're where it most needs buy-in. A technically excellent model can still change nobody's mind. (The Limits to Growth *archetype*, Ch. 13, is a different thing from this study, which borrowed its name.)
