## Situation

A competitor enters a rental market by undercutting the incumbent. The incumbent responds with a deeper discount; the competitor responds again. After three rounds both are renting at a loss; neither planned to.

## Analysis

Two balancing loops, each restoring its owner's relative position, joined by cross-links (each cut lowers the other's relative position) into a reinforcing loop with four minus signs. Unilaterally stopping would only cede the market, because customers keep rewarding the lower price. A later inspection found the competitor had also been the incumbent's own discount supplier for most of a year, through two shell companies: the same relationship wearing two faces.

## Lesson

Fix the comparison, not the willpower: a verifiable floor binding both, or a differentiator neither is competing on. And check whether a competitor is really one legible actor or part of a larger system you haven't bounded yet.
