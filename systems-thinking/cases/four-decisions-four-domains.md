## Situation

Four decisions in one organization, one per domain.

## Analysis

- **Clear**: renewing a known-good insurance policy is a form. The agent calls a written rule.
- **Complicated**: choosing the order of a SARIMA model for a revenue series takes statistical expertise, but a right answer is knowable, so it's checkable: test the agent's forecasts on held-out data before relying on them.
- **Complex**: in month one of a competitor's price war nobody can know how the escalation will unfold, and no amount of advance study would have said. An agent that lists ten cheap price experiments and tracks them is useful; one that announces "the optimal pricing strategy" has misclassified the problem.
- **Chaotic**: during an active incident there's no time to analyze. The agent runs the runbook written in calmer times; it doesn't draft a new one.

## Lesson

The domain, set by a person before the agent answers, decides the agent's job. Treating the price war as a complicated pricing-optimization problem is the most expensive error on the map.
