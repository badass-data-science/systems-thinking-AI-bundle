## Situation

Batches of a processed material keep coming back low-quality because of flaws that arrive with the raw material from extraction. Two fixes are available: hand-inspect each batch, catch bad units and rework them (always available, works within a day); or diagnose and correct the extraction process (slower, harder, politically unpopular with the extraction division).

## Analysis

Every complaint for two years was closed with inspection. Hardly anyone still remembers how to diagnose an extraction defect. The skill wasn't removed; it atrophied, slowly enough that nobody noticed, and each time the fundamental fix was considered it looked harder than before, so inspection won again. (This is a different defect source from the urgency-driven corner-cutting at processing; a real system can have both.)

## Lesson

Keep inspecting, since the complaints are real, but put someone on diagnosing the extraction defect now, while anyone still half-remembers how.
