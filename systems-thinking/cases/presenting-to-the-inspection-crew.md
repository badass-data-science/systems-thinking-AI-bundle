## Situation

For two years an inspection crew had hand-inspected and reworked bad batches, the only reason quality complaints ever got resolved. The analysis said this was Shifting the Burden.

## Analysis

**First attempt**: "This is a classic Shifting the Burden pattern. You've been relying on the symptomatic fix instead of the fundamental one." Accurate; heard as an accusation of incompetence; the room closed.

**Second attempt**: start from the crew's shared frustration at catching the same issue every week, and build the loop live, a link at a time, checking each against their experience. When the capability-erosion loop was on the board, a crew member said unprompted, "wait, so the more we catch by hand, the less anyone remembers how to actually fix the mine side?" That sentence is the archetype.

Boundary critique: the crew moved from informed to consulted; leadership and the union representative were only informed; the extraction division, whose process the fundamental fix would change, hasn't been consulted at all and is the next room.

## Lesson

Name the shape only after the room has named it. Then go to the next room.
